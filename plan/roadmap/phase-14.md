# Phase 14 — Theory of Computation

Sipser chapters 1–5 and 7. What machines can and cannot compute, and what they can compute *efficiently*:
finite automata and regular languages, context-free grammars and pushdown automata, Turing machines,
decidability and the halting problem, and P/NP/NP-completeness. This phase is about the limits every engineer
eventually hits — why no perfect static analyser exists, why some problems have no efficient algorithm, and how
to recognise and respond to them.
**Xref:** DSA owns algorithm *techniques*; this phase owns *computability and complexity theory*. 14.1
(regular languages, DFAs) connects to 12.4.2 (lexing) and 8.3.3 (the TCP state machine as a DFA); 14.2 (CFGs,
PDAs) to 12.4.3 (parsing); 14.3–14.4 (undecidability, NP-hardness) to 12.4.8 (why compilers can't optimise
perfectly) and 13.3.1 (crypto hardness assumptions).
**Reading:** Sipser *Introduction to the Theory of Computation* 3e (SIP) ch 0–5, 7–8, 10.

| 14.3.3 | DD | **The halting problem** — Hook: "Could you write a perfect tool that, given any program and its input, tells you whether it will finish or loop forever? Every developer wants this.
