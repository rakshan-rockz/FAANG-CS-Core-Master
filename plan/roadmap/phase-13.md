# Phase 13 — Security & Cryptography

Threat models and principles; symmetric cryptography (AES, modes, hashes, MACs, AEAD); public-key
cryptography (hard problems, RSA, Diffie–Hellman, elliptic curves, signatures, PKI); protocols (TLS 1.3,
authentication and password storage, network security); defensive study of common vulnerability classes
(memory safety, OWASP Top 10, side channels) so the learner can build systems that resist them; and
**post-quantum cryptography and QKD**, which is the learner's employer's domain (QNu Labs: QKD, QRNG and PQC
products) and a frequent résumé-driven interview topic. Cycle 13.6 is taught to practitioner depth.
**Xref:** SD 1.2.3 (TLS at design level), 1.2.4 & 8.3.1–8.3.2 (web auth, password storage, OAuth/JWT as
designs), 8.3.4 (mTLS, zero trust), 8.4.1 (encryption at rest, KMS, envelope encryption), 8.4.2 (WAF, DDoS,
OWASP), 8.4.3 (STRIDE threat modelling for designs). This phase owns the cryptographic mechanisms and the
defensive understanding of vulnerabilities beneath those decisions. Earlier phases: 1.3.6–1.3.7 (memory
safety), 2.1.3–2.2 (speculation, caches ↔ side channels), 8.1.4 (DNSSEC), 8.3.7 (QUIC), 14.4 (complexity ↔
hardness assumptions).
**Reading:** Aumasson *Serious Cryptography* 2e (SC) 1–14; K&R 8; RFC 8446; FIPS 197/203/204/205; NIST IR 8547;
Bennett & Brassard 1984. **Ethics & scope:** every hands-on exercise runs only against code the learner wrote
or a deliberately vulnerable local target the learner controls; the goal throughout is to *build and defend*,
and to reason about attacks well enough to prevent them.
